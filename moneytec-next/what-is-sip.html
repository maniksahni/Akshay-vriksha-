What is a SIP?

A SIP, or Systematic Investment Plan, is a way to invest a fixed amount in a mutual fund at regular intervals, usually every month. It is not a separate product; it is a way of putting money into a mutual fund scheme.

01

How a SIP works

  1. You pick a scheme, an amount and a date.
  2. You approve an auto-debit from your bank.
  3. On each date, the amount goes to the fund house and you receive units at that day’s NAV.
  4. Your units add up over time, and you can change or stop the SIP later.

02

Rupee cost averaging: a worked example

Because the amount stays fixed, you buy more units when the NAV is lower and fewer when it is higher. Here is ₹1,000 a month for three months, with made-up NAVs:

MonthInvestedNAVUnits bought
1₹1,000₹10.00100
2₹1,000₹8.00125
3₹1,000₹12.5080
Total₹3,000Average cost ₹9.84305

The average cost per unit (₹9.84) is below the simple average of the three NAVs (₹10.17). This averages your buying price over time, but it does not guarantee a profit or prevent a loss.

03

SIP or lump sum?

SIPLump sum
How you investA fixed amount at regular intervalsOne larger amount at once
SuitsRegular incomeMoney you already have
TimingSpread across many datesDepends on one entry date

Neither is always better. In a steadily rising market, a lump sum can do better because the money is invested for longer.

04

Ways to run a SIP

  • Regular SIP: the same amount each month.
  • Step-up SIP: the amount rises at set intervals, for example when your income grows. Where offered, it is a simple way to keep up with a goal.
  • Pause: many fund houses let you pause for a limited time. Terms vary.

05

Three common myths

  • “SIPs always make money.” No. A SIP in a scheme whose value falls can show a loss.
  • “I am locked in.” Usually not, except ELSS, where each instalment is locked for 3 years.
  • “I need a large amount.” Many schemes start at ₹500 a month.

07

Frequently asked questions

What does SIP stand for?

SIP stands for Systematic Investment Plan: investing a fixed amount in a mutual fund at regular intervals.

Is a SIP better than a lump sum?

Not always. A SIP spreads your entry over time and suits regular income, while a lump sum invests existing money at once. The result depends on how markets move.

Is SIP return guaranteed?

No. Returns depend on the scheme and the market, and the value can fall as well as rise.

Can I change my SIP amount?

Usually yes, by stopping the SIP and starting a new one, or using a step-up option where available.
