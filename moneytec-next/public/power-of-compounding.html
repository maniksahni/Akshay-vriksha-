The power of compounding

Compounding means your returns can start earning returns of their own. Over a short period the effect looks small. Over a long period, it can do more of the work than the money you put in.

04

The Rule of 72

Divide 72 by a yearly return to estimate how long money takes to double. At 12%, that is about 6 years; at 6%, about 12 years. It is a rough mental shortcut.

05

What can get in the way

  • Stopping early: the biggest growth tends to come in the later years.
  • Inflation: a larger number in future will buy less than it does today.
  • Market falls: real returns are uneven and can be negative for a long time.
  • Costs and tax: these reduce what stays invested.

All figures here assume a constant return before costs and tax. Real investments do not grow in a straight line, and these are not promises of future returns.

06

Frequently asked questions

What is compounding in simple words?

Earning returns on your earlier returns as well as on the money you originally invested.

Does a mutual fund really compound?

In a growth option, gains stay invested and are reflected in the NAV, so they can build over time. The returns themselves vary and can be negative.

What does the Rule of 72 tell me?

Divide 72 by the yearly return to estimate the years needed to double your money. For example, 12% gives about 6 years.
