ELSS tax-saving funds, explained

An ELSS (Equity Linked Savings Scheme) is an equity mutual fund with a 3-year lock-in. Under the old tax regime, investments have been eligible for a deduction of up to ₹1.5 lakh a year, popularly known as the Section 80C benefit.

01

The key points

  • Lock-in: 3 years from the date of each investment.
  • Tax deduction: available under the old tax regime, within the overall ₹1.5 lakh yearly limit. It is not available under the new regime.
  • Risk: ELSS funds invest mainly in equities, so their value can fall and returns are not guaranteed.

02

How the lock-in works with a SIP

Each SIP instalment has its own 3-year lock-in. It does not all unlock together.

Instalment investedCan be redeemed from
January 2027January 2030
February 2027February 2030
March 2027March 2030

A lump sum, by contrast, is locked for 3 years from the one date you invested.

03

Questions to ask yourself first

  • Which tax regime do you use? Under the new regime there is no ELSS deduction, so tax saving is not a reason to choose it.
  • Have you already used your ₹1.5 lakh limit through EPF, PPF, life insurance premiums or home-loan principal?
  • Can you leave the money invested for at least 3 years, and ideally longer, given the equity risk?

Income-tax law and section numbers change. Confirm the current rules and your own position with a tax professional before you invest for a deduction. Gains on redemption may also be taxable.

04

Frequently asked questions

What is the lock-in period of ELSS?

Three years from the date of each investment. With a SIP, each instalment is locked for its own 3 years.

Can I claim ELSS under the new tax regime?

No. The ELSS deduction is available under the old tax regime, within the overall ₹1.5 lakh limit.

Can I stop an ELSS SIP?

Yes, you can stop future instalments, but money already invested stays locked until each instalment finishes its 3 years.
