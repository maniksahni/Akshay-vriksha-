Mutual fund portfolio review

A portfolio review is a regular check that your SIPs and funds still fit your goals, timeline and comfort with risk. It often means organising what you have, not replacing everything.

01

What gets checked

  • Goals: is each investment linked to a purpose and date?
  • Progress: are you on track at your current amount?
  • Risk: does the overall mix still suit you, especially as a goal gets close?
  • Overlap: do several funds hold much the same investments?
  • Number of funds: can you track what you hold?
  • Costs: expense ratios and any exit loads.
  • Performance in context: against its benchmark and similar funds over longer periods, not just the last few months.
  • Paperwork: nominee, KYC, bank and contact details.

02

What to bring

  • A Consolidated Account Statement (CAS), which shows your mutual fund holdings in one place
  • Your list of SIPs
  • Your goals and when you need the money
  • Any large upcoming expenses

03

How often?

Many people review once a year, and also when something big changes, such as a new job, marriage, a child, a home loan or a move abroad. Checking every week usually makes market noise feel more important than it is.

04

Before you switch anything

Switching is treated as selling one fund and buying another. It can trigger tax and an exit load, and ELSS instalments cannot be redeemed until their 3-year lock-in ends. A recent dip alone is not always a reason to change.

06

Frequently asked questions

How many mutual funds should I hold?

There is no fixed number. It should be few enough to track and varied enough for your goals, so check for overlap between funds.

How often should I review my mutual funds?

Many people review about once a year, and whenever their life or goals change significantly.

What is a Consolidated Account Statement?

A CAS is a single statement of your mutual fund holdings and transactions across fund houses, available through the registrars and depositories.
