What is a mutual fund?

A mutual fund pools money from many investors and invests it in shares, bonds or other assets, as set out in the scheme’s stated objective. A professional fund manager runs it, and each investor owns units in proportion to what they put in.

01

How a mutual fund works

  1. You invest money in a scheme, either in one go or through a SIP.
  2. You receive units based on that day’s price per unit, called the NAV.
  3. The fund manager invests the pooled money according to the scheme’s objective.
  4. The value of your units changes as the value of the investments changes.
  5. You redeem units when you need the money, subject to the scheme’s terms.

02

A simple example

Suppose you invest ₹10,000 when the NAV is ₹50. You receive 200 units. If the NAV later rises to ₹60, your units are worth ₹12,000. If it falls to ₹40, they are worth ₹8,000. These numbers are only an illustration.

03

Main types of mutual funds

SEBI groups open-ended schemes into five broad categories:

TypeWhat it mainly invests inGeneral risk character
Equity fundsCompany sharesHigher ups and downs; usually for longer periods
Debt fundsBonds and money-market instrumentsUsually steadier than equity, but not risk-free
Hybrid fundsA mix of equity and debtDepends on the mix
Solution-oriented fundsBuilt for goals such as retirement or children’s needsDepends on the portfolio; may have a lock-in
Other fundsIndex funds, ETFs and funds of fundsDepends on what they track or hold

ELSS funds are a type of equity fund with a 3-year lock-in.

04

What it costs

  • Expense ratio: a yearly cost, taken from the fund’s assets, that is already reflected in the NAV.
  • Exit load: a charge some schemes apply if you redeem within a set period.
  • Tax: gains can be taxed when you redeem. The rules depend on the fund type and how long you held it.

05

The risks

Mutual funds do not guarantee returns or protect your capital. Market movements, interest rates, credit quality and how long you stay invested can all affect the result. Every scheme shows a riskometer, from Low to Very High, so read that and the scheme documents before you invest.

06

Who protects investors?

Mutual funds in India are regulated by SEBI (Securities and Exchange Board of India). Your money is held by an independent custodian and trustees oversee the fund house, which is separate from your distributor. AMFI is the industry body that registers distributors. Regulation does not remove market risk.

Frequently asked questions

Are mutual funds safe?

They are regulated by SEBI, but they carry market risk. The value of your investment can fall, and returns are not guaranteed. Risk varies by fund type, so check the riskometer and scheme documents.

How much money do I need to start?

Many schemes take SIPs from ₹500 a month. Minimums vary by scheme.

Can I withdraw my money at any time?

Open-ended schemes can generally be redeemed on any business day, but an exit load may apply, and ELSS funds have a 3-year lock-in.

Who manages a mutual fund?

A fund manager at the fund house (AMC) manages the scheme according to its stated objective, under SEBI regulation.
