How to start a SIP in India

A SIP (Systematic Investment Plan) invests a fixed amount in a mutual fund at regular intervals, usually monthly. Many schemes accept SIPs from ₹500 a month, and most can be changed or stopped later.

01

Six steps to your first SIP

  1. Name the goal. Decide what the money is for and roughly when you will need it.
  2. Choose a comfortable amount. Start with what you can keep paying in a difficult month. You can raise it as your income grows. The SIP calculator helps you compare amounts.
  3. Complete KYC. KYC is done once and works across fund houses, mostly online.
  4. Understand the scheme. Read about its risk level, costs, any exit load and the scheme documents.
  5. Set up the auto-debit. Choose a date soon after your salary and approve the bank mandate.
  6. Review it. Check in about once a year, or when your goal or income changes.

02

Documents you usually need

  • PAN
  • Aadhaar (or a passport for NRIs)
  • A bank account in your name
  • A photo

03

Common mistakes

  • Choosing an amount you cannot sustain, then stopping early.
  • Investing money you may need soon in a scheme that can fall in value.
  • Choosing a scheme only because of its recent returns. Past performance may not continue.
  • Forgetting about lock-ins. ELSS funds lock each instalment for 3 years.

A SIP makes investing regular. It does not guarantee a profit or protect against loss in falling markets.

04

Frequently asked questions

What is the minimum amount to start a SIP?

Many schemes accept SIPs from ₹500 a month. Minimums vary, so check the scheme you are considering.

Can I stop or pause my SIP?

Yes. Most SIPs can be paused, changed or stopped any time. ELSS tax-saving funds lock each instalment for 3 years.

What happens if a SIP instalment fails?

The instalment is usually missed for that month and your bank may charge a fee. Repeated failures can lead the fund house to cancel the SIP, so check the terms and keep the balance ready.

Where does my SIP money go?

Straight from your bank account to the fund house (AMC). The units are in your name and the money does not pass through Moneytec.
