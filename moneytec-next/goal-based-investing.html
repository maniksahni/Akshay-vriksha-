Goal-based investing

Goal-based investing starts with what the money is for: a child’s education, a home, retirement. You estimate what it could cost then, how long you have, and how much to set aside. This is how our goal planner on the homepage works.

Four steps

  1. Name the goal and today’s cost.
  2. Set the timeline: the year you will need the money.
  3. Allow for inflation: most goals cost more later.
  4. Work out the monthly amount, counting what you have already saved for it.

A worked example

A goal that costs ₹25 lakh today, 15 years away, with 6% inflation, an assumed 12% yearly return and no existing savings:

Cost today₹25 lakh

Timeline shapes the plan

TimelineTypical goalsWhat to think about
3–5 yearsEmergency fund, short-term goalsAccess to your money, exit conditions and capital risk
5–10 yearsA car, a wedding, a home down-paymentYour target, timeline and a comfortable monthly amount
10–30+ yearsRetirement, children’s education, long-term wealthAffordability, inflation and your ability to stay invested

These are starting points for a conversation, not product recommendations.

As the goal gets closer

A market fall matters more when you need the money soon. Many investors review their plan each year and consider moving towards less volatile options as the date nears, so the goal is less exposed to a late fall.

Why not just invest without a goal?

  • It gives you a number to work towards, instead of a guess.
  • It helps you match your risk to your timeline.
  • You are less tempted to spend money that has a job to do.

Inflation and returns here are assumptions. Your real costs and returns will differ, so review your plan regularly.

Frequently asked questions

What is goal-based investing?

Investing for specific goals, with a target amount and date for each, rather than investing without a defined purpose.

What inflation rate should I assume?

There is no single right number. Our example uses 6%, and education or healthcare costs may rise differently, so try more than one figure.

Should I have a separate SIP for each goal?

Many people find that easier to track, but it is not required. What matters is knowing how much is set aside for each goal.
